Monochrome · one-ink print only

The only place the heart legally stops being jadeite: a medium that physically cannot produce it — one-ink screen print, engraving, fax, laser etch. Not for screens. On screen you always have jadeite available, so an unreadable ground is fixed with a scrim, a keyline, or a plate — see when you can't control the ground.

On photography & busy backgrounds

Scrim the image, then keep the heart jadeite. A scrimmed photo puts jadeite around 6.8:1 — well past the 3:1 WCAG asks of a meaningful graphic — so there's no reason to drop the thread. Fix the ground, not the mark. Drop your own photos in to test. Full working at mono heart on screen.

When you can't control the ground

User-uploaded backgrounds, partner templates, print-on-demand. You can't guarantee a ratio you don't own, so give the heart its own ground instead of recolouring it.

at 44px keyline — holds the silhouette plate — also fine, just heavier
at 16px keyline — 1.5px now eats the shape plate — still legible, still jadeite
controlled ground jadeite, untreated jadeite, untreated

The plate is the only route that keeps the heart jadeite and guarantees the ratio — you own both sides of the contrast. Prefer it. It's solid on purpose: at 84% over a pale photo jadeite drops to 2.2:1, under the bar, so the plate is the one surface in the system that is never glass.
